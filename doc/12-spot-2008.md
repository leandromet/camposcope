# 12 — SPOT 2008 and the legal reference date

Every dataset in this repository is a data source. This one is also a **date**, and that is
why it belongs in Camposcope rather than being one more basemap.

## 1. Why this mosaic, in this app

The Código Florestal (Lei 12.651/2012) defines *área rural consolidada* as an area of a
rural property with anthropic occupation **preexisting 22 July 2008**. That single date is
the hinge of the whole CAR: it separates what a landholder may keep from what must be
restored, and it is the reason a CAR registration records land use at all.

The **Google Brazil Forest Imagery Dataset 2008** is a circa-2008 SPOT mosaic over Brazil's
forest areas — imagery from within months of that cutoff, at 5–10 m, which is finer than
anything else Camposcope has for that period (MapBiomas is 30 m, Landsat 5 is 30 m).

So the mosaic answers a question no other layer in this app can: **what did this property
look like around the date the law cares about?**

That is also exactly the point where Camposcope could stop being a data tool and start
issuing verdicts, so §4 draws the line explicitly.

## 2. The assets

Verified accessible from the `ee-leandromet` service account on **2026-08-23** — both mint
tile URLs and both report their bands:

| | Asset | Bands |
|---|---|---|
| Visual | `GOOGLE/BRAZIL_FOREST_2008/V1/VISUAL` | `R, G, B, date, scale, satellite, coregistered` |
| Analytic | `GOOGLE/BRAZIL_FOREST_2008/V1/ANALYTIC` | `G, R, N, date, scale, satellite, coregistered` |

Visualisation parameters are ported verbatim from Naturametrics' `config/datasets.py`; the
analytic false-colour composite puts near-infrared in the red channel (`N, R, G`), which is
what makes 2008 vegetation legible against pasture at a glance.

**Licence-gated.** The dataset requires accepting the "Brazil Forest Imagery Dataset 2008"
agreement, granted per service account. `CS_SPOT_ENABLED` is the kill switch for a
deployment whose account lacks the grant: the layers are **dropped from the panel entirely**
rather than offered and then failing. An option that never works is worse than an option
that is not there.

**Partial coverage is the dataset, not a failure.** The catalogue describes the mosaic as
covering Brazil's forest areas. Measured, the picture is more uneven than that description
suggests — see the table in §3 — with coverage ranging from **100 % to nothing at all**. A
property with no SPOT coverage must be told so, not shown an empty map.

## 3. The `date` band — the part that makes this honest

The mosaic is *circa* 2008. Individual scenes were acquired across a window, and `date`
carries the **per-pixel acquisition time as Unix epoch seconds**.

Measured over the test property (MT-5108501-CBE0F5…, Vera/MT, 5 km neighbourhood):

| | Epoch | Decoded |
|---|---|---|
| earliest pixel | 1 211 465 123 | **2008-05-22** |
| latest pixel | 1 213 192 137 | **2008-06-11** |
| cutoff | 1 216 684 800 | 2008-07-22 |

Both before the cutoff — **for this property**. Generalising from that single sample would
have been a serious mistake. Sampling ~10 km boxes across six regions on 2026-08-23:

| Region | Coverage | Acquisition range | **Before 22/07/2008** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sertão / PI | 100 % | 2008-03-11 → 2008-07-01 | **100 %** |
| Vera / MT *(test property)* | 100 % | 2008-05-22 → 2008-06-11 | **100 %** |
| Amazônia / AM | 74 % | 2008-07-14 | **100 %** |
| Cerrado agrícola / GO | 100 % | 2008-04-12 → 2008-07-26 | **45 %** |
| Caatinga / BA | 67 % | 2008-05-17 → 2008-11-16 | **26 %** |
| Litoral / SP | 35 % | 2008-10-11 | **0 %** |
| Pantanal / MS | **0 %** | — | — |

Read that last column again. Over the Litoral SP box, **every** SPOT pixel was acquired in
October 2008 — *after* the legal reference date. Over the Cerrado agricultural belt, more
than half were. An app that showed this mosaic labelled "2008" and left it there would be
inviting users to read post-cutoff imagery as evidence of pre-cutoff occupation, in exactly
the region where the stakes are highest.

The mosaic is *circa* 2008 in the honest sense of the word, and the `date` band is the only
thing that makes it usable for the question the CAR actually asks.

Camposcope therefore **never** shows the SPOT layer without also computing, for the
property's own extent:

- the **date range** of the pixels actually covering it (`date` min/max, decoded);
- the **fraction of covered pixels acquired before 2008-07-22**;
- the **fraction with no coverage at all**.

Those three numbers go in the layer panel next to the toggle and into the provenance block
of any export that includes the layer. A SPOT screenshot without them is a picture that
implies a date it may not have.

`satellite` (SPOT 4 vs 5) and `scale` (5 m vs 10 m) are carried into provenance for the same
reason, cheaply, since they come from the same reducer call.

## 4. Where the line is (constraints C4 and D9)

**Permitted** — showing data and saying what it is:

- displaying the mosaic, with its acquisition dates;
- stating that *n* % of the property's pixels were imaged before 2008-07-22;
- placing 2008 beside 1985 and 2024 in a Sankey ([07](07-transitions.md)) as a year like any
  other;
- letting the user look at their own property in 2008 and draw their own conclusions.

**Forbidden** — the same data with a verdict attached:

- labelling any area "consolidada" or "não consolidada";
- computing an *área rural consolidada* figure, in hectares or percent;
- deriving a *passivo ambiental*, a restoration obligation, or a compliance status;
- any wording that implies Camposcope has determined what the law permits on this property.

The distinction is not pedantic. *"This land was already cleared in the 2008 imagery"* is an
observation. *"This is consolidated area"* is a legal classification that depends on facts
Camposcope cannot see — the date of the occupation rather than of the photograph, the
property's status at the time, subsequent decisions — and that only a competent authority
makes. Decision **D9** forbids the second, and this dataset is precisely where the
temptation to cross the line is strongest.

The layer therefore carries its own permanent note, in the same register as the cadastral
disclosure:

> Imagens SPOT de aproximadamente 2008, referência visual para a data de corte do Código
> Florestal (22/07/2008). A data de aquisição varia por pixel e é informada acima.
> **Camposcope não classifica área consolidada nem avalia regularidade.**

## 5. Implementation

- `config/datasets.py::EE_BASEMAPS` — the two entries, ported, gated on `CS_SPOT_ENABLED`.
- `services/layers.py` — tile URLs minted through the existing `tiles.py` cache; SPOT is an
  Earth Engine basemap, so it is switched on by a background event rather than a plain state
  write (a tile URL has to be minted per session).
- `services/spot.py` — the coverage/date reducer of §3, returning
  `(dict, Provenance)` like every other analysis ([02](02-architecture.md) §6). One
  `reduceRegion` over the property, `bestEffort=True`, at 100 m: the question is "when was
  this imaged", not "what colour is this pixel", and 100 m answers it for a fraction of the
  cost.
- Phase 5 in [03-roadmap.md](03-roadmap.md), alongside the other imagery work.

**Done test:** the SPOT layer draws over the test property; the panel states the acquisition
date range, the pre-cutoff fraction and the uncovered fraction; a property in the Pantanal
box (0 % coverage) says so instead of showing an empty map; a property in the Litoral SP box
reports **0 % pre-cutoff** rather than implying the imagery predates the reference date; and
no string anywhere in the UI or an export contains the word "consolidada" as a
classification of area.

`tests/test_spot_live.py` pins the six sampled regions above as an opt-in live test — not to
assert exact percentages, which may shift if the asset is revised, but to catch the mosaic
silently becoming uniform or the `date` band changing units.
